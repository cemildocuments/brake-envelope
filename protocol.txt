Working topic: Calibrating Continuous-Speed Braking
Decision date: 2026-11-19. Protocol fixed before confirmatory simulations.
Research question: Can the fractional-linear normalized residual of a delay-plus-constant-deceleration stopping law be calibrated at two endpoints to protect all speeds and adaptive clearance-based speed selection, without a discretization penalty?
Novelty boundary: functional supremum conformal bands, split conformal ranks, tolerance bounds and safe speed constraints are established. The intended contribution is the exact braking-specific endpoint reduction, its explicit safe-speed map and a controlled comparison of safety/efficiency and failure under model/operating shift. No claim of first conformal controller or new general conformal theorem.
Target model: D(v)=tau*v+beta*v^2, beta=1/(2a), v in [0,3] m/s. Stop is at v=0. Episode has fixed latent tau,beta. All speed responses share the same episode parameters. Context-conditioned estimates and scales use independent training and scale-fitting folds. One conformal score per episode. Available clearance is exact and static.
Main alpha .05; n calibration=511; training=4000; scale fitting=2000; testing=10000 per split. Four synthetic parameter populations: correlated Gaussian, negatively correlated heteroscedastic Gaussian, heavy-tailed t5, and bimodal delay/brake faults. Independent seeds 1000-1039 (40 per population). Development seed 7 is excluded from confirmatory analyses.
Comparators: nominal; random-speed pooled normalized conformal; two-endpoint maximum; endpoint with separate coordinate scales disabled; two coefficient-wise Bonferroni bounds; 21-knot Bonferroni envelope using convex secants; endpoint tolerance rank with alpha=.05,delta=.05. Also a held-out frozen-policy direct conformal calibration baseline, if computationally feasible, calibrated by the smallest inflation that makes the selected speed safe for each episode.
Primary outcomes: probability the entire stopping curve exceeds its band; clearance violation of selected speed; mean speed/clairvoyant feasible speed; conservative fractional speed loss max(oracle-v,0)/oracle. Report paired differences with 95% t intervals across independent splits. Do not pool test episodes as independent for inferential uncertainty when they share a calibration set.
Secondary settings: alpha .01,.02,.05,.10; n=63,127,255,511,1023,2047; random clearances log-uniform [.08,4.5] meters; low-clearance [.03,.4]; high-clearance [.8,4.5]. Full curve supremum is analytic, not numerically approximated. Train/test data and operation are stationary in primary experiments.
Stress tests: unseen 25% inverse-deceleration shift; nonquadratic cubic stopping term calibrated quadratically; include an explicit shape-corrected benchmark using a known cubic upper bound to demonstrate the cost of misspecification. These violate main theorem assumptions; report failure honestly, without claiming distribution-shift robustness.
Validation: exact endpoint identity against dense speed sweep; dynamics integration against analytic stopping distance; no data overlap; quantile sentinel for insufficient calibration; no negative speed; proof of containment implies one-stop safety. Reproduce aggregate numerical results from preserved seeds and code. Tests are for scientific invariants, not cosmetic code behavior.
Data are synthetic benchmark episodes, not sensor data or physical-robot observations. No real-world safety certification, conditional subgroup guarantee, or multi-stop lifetime guarantee is claimed.

Implementation audit clarification: the grid comparator has 21 knots including the deterministic origin; its Bonferroni budget is allocated only to the 20 positive knots. This correction changes only a secondary large-calibration-size setting, not the primary comparison. All output tables and plots use the corrected implementation.
